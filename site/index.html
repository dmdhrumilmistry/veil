Features

Everything you need. Nobody in the middle.

Veil is built around one idea: the only devices that should ever see your conversation are yours and theirs.

Truly peer-to-peer

Messages travel over WebRTC data channels directly between devices. No server stores or relays them.

Post-quantum handshake

Hybrid X25519 + ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203) key exchange, so recorded traffic stays protected against future quantum attacks.

Forward secrecy

Fresh ephemeral keys every session and a per-message key chain. Each message key is deleted after use.

Offline mailbox (optional)

Leave messages for contacts who are away. A self-hosted relay holds only padded, Double Ratchet encrypted envelopes and wakes them with an empty push.

Encrypted backups

Export everything to one encrypted file you control, and restore it on a new device. No cloud account involved.

Verified contacts

Compare a 60-digit safety number or scan a QR code. Veil warns you if a contact's identity key ever changes.

Fast file sharing

Send files of any size in 64 KiB encrypted chunks on a dedicated channel, each file SHA-256 verified on arrival.

Encrypted at rest

Everything stays on your device in IndexedDB, encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key protected by your passphrase.

Lock, expire, wipe

Auto-lock when you step away, disappearing messages, and a one-tap wipe that removes everything from the device.

Hardened by default

Strict Content-Security-Policy and Trusted Types. No third-party scripts, no analytics, no trackers, no cookies.

Installable everywhere

A progressive web app for desktop, Android, iOS and tablets. Install it from your browser in a couple of taps.

How it works

Connected in three steps

There is no server to introduce you, so you introduce yourselves. Share codes over any channel you like - in person, or through another messenger.

  1. 1

    Create an invite

    Veil generates an invite link and QR code that carries your identity key. Show the QR in person or send the link however you like.

  2. 2

    They open it and reply

    Your contact opens the link in Veil, which produces a reply code. They send that code back to you through the same or any other channel.

    veil-reply: 7Hq2-xP9k-Lm4V...
  3. 3

    Connected - now verify

    Paste the reply and your devices connect directly. Compare safety numbers to confirm nobody is in between, then start chatting.

    Safety numbers match

Each new session needs a fresh invite exchange. That is the honest trade-off of having no server to rendezvous through.

Security deep-dive

Two layers of encryption, zero trust in the network

Veil runs its own authenticated, post-quantum end-to-end encryption inside WebRTC's mandatory DTLS encryption. Even if one layer failed, the other still stands.

Handshake Hybrid post-quantum key exchange

X25519 is combined with ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203). An attacker would need to break both to recover session keys - including one recording traffic today to decrypt later.

Identity Mutual authentication

Both sides sign the full handshake transcript with their Ed25519 identity keys, bound to the WebRTC DTLS certificate fingerprints so the encrypted channel cannot be swapped out underneath.

Messages Symmetric ratchet

Keys are derived with HKDF-SHA-256 and advanced through a Signal-style KDF chain. Each message and file chunk is sealed with AES-256-GCM under a one-time key that is deleted after use.

Storage Encrypted at rest

Local data in IndexedDB is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a random data key, wrapped by a key derived from your passphrase with PBKDF2-SHA-256 at 600,000 iterations.

Browser Locked-down runtime

A strict Content-Security-Policy and Trusted Types block injected scripts. No third-party code is loaded - ever.

Works everywhere

Install it like an app

Veil is a progressive web app. First open the app (this page is just the website), then add it to your device and launch it from your home screen or dock.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Open the app in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button.
  3. Choose Add to Home Screen.

Android

  1. Open the app in your browser.
  2. Tap the menu (three dots).
  3. Chrome: choose Install app. Firefox: choose Add app to Home screen. Samsung Internet: choose Add page to, then Home screen.

Desktop

  1. Open the app in Chrome, Edge or Safari.
  2. Chrome/Edge: click the install icon in the address bar. Safari: File, then Add to Dock.
  3. Confirm. Firefox on desktop cannot install web apps yet.

FAQ

Questions, answered honestly

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up, no phone number and no email. Your identity is a key pair generated on your device.

Why do I have to exchange a new invite every session?

Because there is no server to rendezvous through. Cloud messengers can reconnect you automatically because their servers know where you are. Veil deliberately has no such server, so each new live session starts with a fresh invite and reply exchange over a channel you choose. If your contact runs a mailbox relay, you can still leave them encrypted messages in between.

Does Veil use any servers at all?

Only to help devices find each other on the network. Public STUN servers tell your device its public address - they never see message content. You can disable STUN in settings and configure your own TURN relay instead. A TURN relay forwards traffic but only ever sees ciphertext. Nothing stores or relays your messages by default.

Why won't it connect on some networks?

Some strict networks, such as those using symmetric NAT, block direct peer-to-peer connections. In that case you will need to configure a TURN relay in settings so traffic can be forwarded. Your messages remain end-to-end encrypted through it.

What does "post-quantum" mean here?

The handshake combines classic X25519 with ML-KEM-768, the post-quantum key encapsulation mechanism standardized in FIPS 203. Session keys stay safe as long as either one holds, which protects against someone recording traffic today and decrypting it with a future quantum computer.

How do I know I am talking to the right person?

Compare your 60-digit safety number, or scan each other's QR code in person. Invite links also carry the sender's identity key, so scanning an invite face to face authenticates them. If a contact's identity key ever changes, Veil warns you.

Can I send large files?

Yes, files of any size. They are streamed in 64 KiB encrypted chunks over a dedicated data channel with backpressure, so chat never blocks behind a transfer, and every file is verified with SHA-256 when it arrives.

What happens if I forget my passphrase?

Your local data is encrypted under a key that only your passphrase can unlock, and there is no account or server that could reset it. That is why Veil offers encrypted backups: create one in Settings, keep the file somewhere safe, and you can restore your identity, contacts and chats on any device - even if you forget your device passphrase.

Why doesn't the app on my iPhone show my Safari chats?

On iPhone and iPad, apps added to the Home Screen get their own storage, completely separate from Safari, so they act as two different installations. Install Veil before you start chatting, or move your data: in Safari open Settings, create a backup, then choose "Restore from a backup" in the Home Screen app. On Android and desktop, the installed app and the browser share the same data, and Veil runs in one window at a time to keep it in sync.

Do we have to be online at the same time to connect?

Not if you use a mailbox relay. Your invite becomes a single link that stays valid for 7 days. Your friend opens it whenever they like, and the two apps connect on their own the next time you are both online - no reply code to send back. Contacts you already have reconnect with one tap. Without a relay, Veil falls back to one-time codes that work while both screens are open.

Can I message someone who is offline?

Yes, if they set up a mailbox relay - a tiny service anyone can run with one Docker command. Your message is encrypted with the Signal Double Ratchet (seeded from Veil's post-quantum handshake), padded, and left in their mailbox with no sender id. The relay only ever holds ciphertext and deletes it once collected. It can also wake their device with an empty push notification. Without a relay, Veil stays purely peer-to-peer.

How do backups work without a cloud?

Veil writes a single .veilbackup file that you keep wherever you like - a USB drive, your own cloud storage, another computer. It is encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a backup passphrase (PBKDF2-SHA-256, 600,000 iterations) and sealed record by record, so tampering or a half-copied file is detected before anything is restored. Photos and files are optional. Nobody can open it without the backup passphrase.

Does Veil track me?

No. There are no analytics, no trackers, no cookies and no third-party scripts - in the app or on this site. The source code is open for anyone to check.
